# Dataset Information

## Dataset Used

**OpenAQ Air Quality Dataset (Del Norte, New Mexico)**

The dataset contains air quality measurements collected from the Del Norte monitoring station in New Mexico.

The analysis focused on:

- PM10
- PM2.5
- Ozone (O3)
- Carbon Monoxide (CO)
- Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2)
- Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)

The dataset was used to explore environmental pollution patterns and demonstrate AI-assisted analytical workflows using structured prompts and AI-generated insights.

# Key Insights

- PM10 and PM2.5 were the dominant pollutants throughout the monitoring period.
- Significant pollution spikes occurred during mid-to-late December.
- Pollution levels peaked during afternoon and evening hours.
- PM10 and PM2.5 showed a strong positive correlation, suggesting related pollution sources.
- O3 demonstrated weak relationships with particulate pollutants.
- SO2 levels remained consistently negligible across the dataset.
- AI-assisted analysis simplified trend identification and statistical interpretation.

---

# Correlation Analysis Findings

The correlation analysis revealed several important environmental relationships:

- PM10 and PM2.5 showed the strongest positive relationship, indicating that both pollutants may originate from similar environmental or combustion-related sources.
- Carbon Monoxide (CO) displayed moderate positive relationships with particulate matter, suggesting shared pollution activity during high-emission periods.
- Ozone (O3) demonstrated weaker relationships with particulate pollutants, reflecting different atmospheric formation processes.
- Sulfur Dioxide (SO2) remained constant at near-zero levels and therefore showed minimal analytical variation.

The analysis helped improve understanding of pollutant interaction patterns and strengthened the overall environmental interpretation of the dataset.
