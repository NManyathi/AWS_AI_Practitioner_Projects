# OpenAQ Air Quality Analysis Using Amazon PartyRock

## Case Study Overview

This case study demonstrates how Amazon PartyRock was used to perform AI-assisted environmental data analysis using the OpenAQ air quality dataset.

The project focused on identifying pollutant trends, detecting pollution spikes, generating statistical summaries, analyzing pollutant relationships through correlation analysis, and evaluating the effectiveness of AI-assisted analytical workflows.

# Prompt Engineering Strategy

The analysis used structured prompts to improve the quality and relevance of AI-generated outputs.

## Prompt 1 – Pollutant Overview

```text
What are the main air pollutants recorded in this dataset and which pollutants appear most frequently?
```

### Purpose

To identify the dominant pollutants recorded in the dataset and understand their measurement distribution.

---

## Prompt 2 – Time Trend Analysis

```text
How do air quality measurements change over time across the dataset?
```

### Purpose

To analyze how pollution levels changed throughout the monitoring period and identify increasing or decreasing trends.

---

## Prompt 3 – Peak Pollution Analysis

```text
Which dates recorded the highest pollution levels and what patterns can be observed?
```

### Purpose

To identify periods with extreme pollution concentrations and evaluate pollution patterns.

---

## Prompt 4 – Correlation Analysis

```text
Are there any noticeable relationships between different air pollutants in the dataset?
```

### Purpose

To examine whether increases in one pollutant were associated with increases in other pollutants and identify possible shared environmental sources or pollution behaviors.

---

## Prompt 5 – Statistical Summary Table

```text
Generate a summary analysis table showing average, minimum, and maximum values for each air pollutant.
```

### Purpose

To summarize pollutant concentration ranges and compare pollutant variability.

# Key Findings

## Major Pollutants

- PM10 and PM2.5 were the dominant pollutants recorded.
- Particulate matter showed significantly higher variability than gaseous pollutants.

## Pollution Trends

- Pollution levels increased significantly during mid-to-late December.
- The most critical pollution period occurred between December 17–19, 2025.

## Daily Pollution Patterns

- Pollution concentrations were highest during afternoon and evening hours.
- Peak pollution periods generally occurred between 3:00 PM and 6:00 PM.

## Correlation Analysis Insights

- PM10 and PM2.5 showed similar pollution behavior patterns during peak pollution periods.
- Increased particulate matter concentrations were often accompanied by increases in gaseous pollutants such as CO and NO2.
- The observed relationships suggest possible shared pollution sources including traffic emissions, winter heating activities, and atmospheric stagnation conditions.
- SO2 remained consistently negligible and showed no meaningful relationship with other pollutants.

## Low Sulfur Dioxide Levels

- SO2 concentrations remained consistently negligible throughout the dataset.

## AI-Assisted Insights

- AI-generated statistical summaries simplified trend interpretation.
- Structured prompts improved the quality of analytical outputs.
- Correlation analysis improved understanding of pollutant interactions during high pollution periods.

# Correlation Analysis Summary

The correlation analysis revealed that particulate pollutants such as PM10 and PM2.5 tended to increase together during major pollution events.

Moderate relationships were also observed between particulate matter and gaseous pollutants such as CO and NO2, suggesting that multiple pollutants may originate from related environmental or combustion-based activities.

The analysis demonstrated how AI-assisted workflows can help identify hidden pollutant relationships more efficiently and support deeper environmental interpretation.
